# Jherbalz — Everything local. Unless you want it.

I'm not an engineer. I'm an artist with an engineering heart.

Twenty years of dabbling in tech, no formal degree — just a habit of taking things apart to see what breaks and which *what ifs* survive. The ideas were never the problem. **Finishing** was. I'd get halfway, the unanswered what-ifs would pile up higher than my knowledge, and the project would quietly die. That was the pattern for two decades.

The pressure that finally broke it came from two places. Twin autistic boys — Malcolm and Marcus — mathematical geniuses, obsessed with animation and Roblox. Malcolm designs his own games; I haven't bought Robux in years. And my rule as their dad: *if I build a game asset maker, it has to be something a child — or anyone on the spectrum — could understand and run without help.*

So everything I build follows three rules:

- **Everything local.** Your machine, your files, your GPU. No cloud — unless you want it.
- **One download, one button, works the first time.**
- **Free. Stays free.**

Agentic workflows were the missing piece I'd been waiting my whole life for. The station below is the first thing to come out the other end.

The same brief is growing into a toy company — **HERBERTO MALMARS** — spectrum-friendly toys, designed with the same rules and printed at home.

For context, not sympathy: my wife is a cancer survivor, we both work commission, I'm 49 — from Brooklyn, living in Philly — and this gets built between school runs and homework. That's terrain, not tragedy — reading the terrain and routing around it is the one skill I've actually mastered.

Everything local. Unless you want it.
